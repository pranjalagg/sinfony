sinfony

Piece

Tonal: classical piano; chords pull toward a home chord, and the piece ends there. Ambient: slow and drifting; chords glide to close neighbors while two bell loops repeat.

ⓘ the math

Tonal: chords follow a Markov chain over functions, Tonic → Subdominant → Dominant → Tonic. The melody is a 2-bar motif, repeated and moved to follow your drawing. Ambient: chords walk the Tonnetz with P, L, R moves (each changes one note by one or two semitones). The loops have 8 and 13 steps, so they line up again only every lcm(8, 13) = 104 steps.

Which notes the music may use. The first menu picks the home note; the second picks the mood (major sounds bright, minor darker).

ⓘ the math

A scale is a set of pitch classes in ℤ₁₂. Major = 7 stacked fifths: 7k mod 12 gives {0, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11}. A mode uses the same notes as another scale with a different home note.

How fast, in beats per minute.

ⓘ the math

One beat lasts 60 / bpm seconds. The performance pushes a little faster in the middle of each 4-bar phrase, using the arc 1 − 4(u − ½)², and slows down over the final bars.

Tune

Higher: the tune moves to nearby notes. Lower: it's free to leap. You hear it most when Wiggle or your drawing jumps around.

ⓘ the math

Each candidate note c gets a score |c − target| + w · max(0, |c − previous| − 2), with w = smoothness. A whole step (2 semitones) is free; every semitone beyond it costs w. Tonal picks the lowest score; Ambient picks at random, with p(c) ∝ exp(−score / 0.8), so low scores are much likelier.

How far the tune wanders from your drawing. 0 stays close to it; 1 wanders a lot.

ⓘ the math

shape(t) = drawing(t) + wiggle · pink(t), clipped to [0, 1]. Pink (1/f) noise has big slow drifts and small fast jitters, like real melodies (Voss & Clarke, 1975).

How many notes the tune plays. Low: a few long notes. High: busy.

ⓘ the math

At every grid step (½ beat in Tonal, 1 beat in Ambient) a note starts with probability density × beat strength. Strong beats get notes first.

Chance

The piece is fixed until you change this number. The same seed gives the same piece on every computer. A new seed also picks a new key and tempo.

ⓘ the math

All randomness comes from a generator started from this number. Each layer (plan, harmony, melody…) gets its own stream, so changing one control doesn't reshuffle the others.

Melody shape

Draw where the tune goes high or low. Sections with the same letter (A, A′) repeat, so drawing one draws them all.

ⓘ the math

target pitch = low + shape(t) · (high − low), inside the melody's range. Notes are then picked by the score under Smoothness. In Tonal, the first 2 bars become a motif, and each later copy of it is moved up or down to sit where you drew.

Tension

Low: calm and softer. High: louder and more intense; in Tonal, a few more chords that want to resolve.

ⓘ the math

Loudness × (0.7 + 0.3 · tension). Tonal: when a new chord is chosen, the chance of a Dominant × (1 + tension), of a Tonic × (1.5 − tension). Repeated sections reuse their first chords, so tension changes chords mostly in sections heard once (like B). Ambient: the chance of a chord change = 0.3 + 0.6 · tension.
